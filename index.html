When clients call

Situations I am asked to resolve.

Most assignments begin with one of these. Each has a practical way through.

01

A new facility must open, but systems and staff are not ready.

A phased opening plan, rosters, SOPs and day-one reporting — so you start serving patients and earning on schedule, not paying salaries for empty wards.

02

An inspection or licence renewal is due.

A gap assessment against PHC/MSDS standards, a committee with clear owners and a dated workplan — so you walk into the inspection with evidence, not promises.

03

Revenue does not cover costs, and no one can say why.

Cost per patient and per service, package prices checked against real costs — so you see which services lose money and set recovery targets the board can track.

04

The board receives figures nobody trusts.

One daily record, cross-checked against billing, cash and registers — so decisions rest on numbers that hold up when questioned.

05

Programme targets are slipping.

District supervision, data and accountability rebuilt around the indicators that count — so funders see progress, not explanations.

06

Staff costs and disputes keep rising.

Merit-based pay, clear attendance rules and an HR policy staff understand — so you keep skilled people and spend payroll where it earns its keep.

Practice

Two levels of the health system, one practice.

Whether you run one hospital or a district programme, the work joins up: facility operations on one side, programme delivery on the other. Few advisers have managed both.

For hospitals, trust and private facilities, and outsourced public facilities.
Planning and commissioning
Service mix, staffing and equipment plans, phased opening and licensing — so the facility opens on time and on budget.
Operations and management systems
Reporting, dashboards, billing and revenue controls, pharmacy and stores — so managers see problems the day they start.
Quality, licensing and patient safety
PHC/MSDS readiness, infection prevention, complaints and incident systems, clinical audit — so standards are met and can be shown.
Finance and costing
Cost per patient and per service, tariff review, board reporting — so pricing and subsidy decisions rest on real costs.
Workforce
Organisation design, merit-based pay, HR policy, attendance and payroll — so good staff stay and payroll stays in control.
Turnaround and interim leadership
Rapid diagnostic, recovery plan and hands-on administration — so a struggling service stabilises quickly.
For government departments, development partners and NGOs.
Maternal, newborn and child health
Family planning, immunisation and nutrition programmes — run to the indicators funders measure.
Public–private partnerships
Outsourcing of primary health facilities, contract management and oversight — so outsourced services deliver what was paid for.
Health insurance programmes
Facility performance against universal health insurance targets — so enrolled patients are served and claims are earned.
District health management
Supervision systems, surveillance and service-delivery targets — so district teams know where they stand each month.
Mobile and community services
Design and launch of outreach and community clinic models — so services reach people who do not reach facilities.
Monitoring and assessment
Data systems, baseline and progress assessments, partner reporting — so results can be evidenced.
